- **Silence closes the gate's own senses** — `Investigating`. Under `silence_threshold` the gate returns early: it zeroes the sustain and NHWRSF metrics, clears `transient_active` and the stable counter, and never reaches the spectral flux, so `prev_spectrum` keeps whatever the last audible hop left in it. Three consequences, none yet measured. The first audible hop after any quiet stretch is compared against a stale spectrum, so its flux is meaningless — it usually clears the threshold anyway, since a strike is loud. The metrics read zero in silence rather than reading the room, so nothing observes the ambient level the gates are supposed to be set against, which is the same gap the noise-floor entry above describes from the other side. And the sustain EMA restarts from zero at every note rather than from where the room sits. Keep the silence test ahead of the onset test whatever changes: a room louder than the threshold must not be able to report a strike. Moving any of this moves every lock baseline, so it needs a `gatekeeper dump` replay over all three capture sets first.
- **The transient is not the wait** — resolved by measurement. The gate's four-hop stable run was the wait, and the lock's vote no longer sits it out: the window fills from the onset, every measured commit still lands on a `Stable` frame, and the lock arrives a median 42–48 ms sooner on piano #2, never later, non-inferior on every instrument read. The capture still starts its record on `Stable`.
  → [report 0020](reports/0020-vote-from-onset.md)
- **The gate's onset and bypass flags are one signal** — `Investigating`. `Gatekeeper::process_transient_detection` raises `is_new_onset` on exactly the frames it returns as the bypass, so `GateResult`'s two flags are always equal, and `Engine::process`'s `is_new_onset` branch never runs, because the bypass returns first. Either they were meant to differ or one goes; removing one changes `Engine::process`'s signature, which no longer takes `is_stable` (report 0020), and the lab harnesses that pass both.
- **No noise floor; the silence threshold stands in for one** — `Planned`. Nothing measures the room's noise. RMS calibration sets the silence threshold to the loudest ambient RMS it reads × 1.5, and three detectors use that one broadband number as their noise σ: discovery's peak floor, which every lock baseline rests on; the tracker's per-partial gate, which seeds MAT; and the strobe bank's per-reference gate. As a σ it is right nowhere, about 4× too low in the bass and 16–20× too high in the top octave. A per-bin floor recorded at startup is ruled out (it fails its false-alarm guard), and a local-reference (CFAR) gate, already shipping in the coarse read, has no reference cells in the bass, so a replacement must answer the bass separately. Replacing the gates moves every lock baseline. The same work retires the `noise_floor` name (the capture key, `engine.noise_floor`, the strobe's argument, and in the GUI `NoiseFloorSettings`, `DEFAULT_NOISE_MULTIPLIER`, `ToggleNoiseFloorAdjustment` and `RecalibrateNoiseFloor`) and the lab's fixed values in place of the silence threshold: 0.001 in `strobe truth`, `strobe readout` and the `gates ab` ambient row, 0.005 in `gates pfa`, 0.003 in `strobe isolation`.
  → [report 0015](reports/retiring/0015-ambient-sigma-gates-measured.md)

## Worker and measurement

- **`key_index` is not a sufficient measurement identity on every instrument** — `Deferred`, and only matters once a non-piano workflow exists. A fretted note is producible on several strings of different gauge and speaking length, hence different $B$; a piano note is 1–3 strings. A per-string/course discriminator on `KeyMeasurement` is the obvious shape, and costs nothing to add later — an additive `#[serde(default)]` field needs no migration, which is why one was *not* reserved speculatively.

- **The precision blend is withheld** — `Gated on` instruments outside the eight analysed data sets, and on the few-partial recording bias (below). The tuning curve's B is the instrument's fitted curve, Rigaud's B_ξ̂ on the bass-bridge keys, for every key. The blend of each key's measured B with it, w = σ_p²/(σ_p² + σ_m²), stays in the code for engines (b) and (c) and the lab, and no app setting reaches it. Five measurements found no form of it that holds. The partial-count law σ_m(n) = max(19.3·n⁻³, 0.0035) does not calibrate across instruments. Each key's scatter is its own, and modelling it per key calibrates within a sitting but moves the curve by under 0.04 ¢. Few-partial B shifts between sittings, where from eight partials up the sittings agree to one or two percent. And a gross capture, B more than ten times off the fitted curve, enters at full weight: the RWC Steinway's D1, over 500 times the curve with 20 partials, gave engine (d) a 70 ¢ draw-to-draw SD at that key. A band a decade wide around the curve removes exactly the gross captures, and it waits with the blend. With the interval rows held fixed, swapping the blend for the fitted curve moves the default curve by 0.09–0.33 ¢ on the instruments where per-key structure reproduced. Bringing the blend back needs a weighting that calibrates on new instruments, since every rule so far was learned on the eight, and the recording bias explained.
  → [report 0009](reports/0009-repeat-capture-noise-decomposition.md), [report 0022](reports/0022-curve-bass-bridge-fit.md), [report 0024](reports/0024-curve-fitted-b-default.md)
- **Few-partial B moves with the recording, not only with the string** — `Investigating`. Below eight partials, piano #2's extended sitting reads B about a fifth lower than its July sitting on average across keys, a gap no tuning change explains (30 ¢ of tension moves B by under 4 %); from eight partials up the two sittings agree to one or two percent. On the external sets a key's captures scatter 1.4 to 2 times as widely as the partial-count law predicts on the Iowa and Disklavier recordings, and less than it predicts on the RWC ones, though all three record the same three dynamics, so the dynamic alone is not the cause. The candidates are the room, the microphone and the noise floor, acting on how many partials the estimator finds and how it numbers them, which may make it the noise-floor work's to fix (*No noise floor*, under Engine and discovery). One more: harmonic distortion, whose peaks read as B ≈ 0 (next entry); a sitting that recorded more of it would read lower below the floor. It is the strongest reason the curve should not lean on few-partial captures.
- **Treble distortion peaks read as partials** — `Planned`. Every one of piano #2's fourteen captures whose B lies more than ten times off its own curve is a treble key from A♯6 up, reading 14 to 570 times too low, and they recur on the same keys: A♯6 in six captures across both sittings, G♯7 in five. That is what report 0005 measured on this piano: the treble contaminant is harmonic distortion at exact multiples of the fundamental (37 : 3), and a peak there reads as B ≈ 0. The remedy report 0005 names, mPFD's front-end structure aimed at n·f₁ (predict the distortion's frequencies, drop candidate peaks near them, keep the strongest survivor), costs no new constant and was never tried. Nothing checks a finished capture's partials today; the red ✗ reads only its B (*The bad-capture check is one-sided*, under Known limitations).
  → [report 0005](reports/0005-discovery-algorithm-class.md)
- **A misread bass capture puts a false beat on the strobe** — `Planned`. The strobe's references for a measured key follow that string's own series, f_n = n·f₀·√(1 + B·n²) with the key's measured B, as they must. In the bass and tenor it shows partials 2 to 8, so a misread B there marks a correctly tuned partial as beating; the treble shows the fundamental only and is immune. Piano #2 has no bass misread since the seed gate; the external sets do, 33 to 525 times too high. The red ✗ now marks them (report 0024), but the strobe still reads the misread B. Candidate: treat a key whose B lies more than ten times off the piano's curve as unmeasured on the strobe (fundamental only), and ask for a recapture.
- **The strobe's bass targets assume one B per string** — `Investigating`. Each reference comes from the single-B law, so zeroing the displayed partial puts the string's fitted series off the curve's target by however far that partial sits from the law, and the coarse and fine reads disagree by as much. In the bass it is not on it. Fitted on partials 2 and up, piano #2's bass partials sit a median 3.4, 2.7 and 1.0 ¢ off at partials 2, 4 and 8, the Disklavier's as far, and the RWC and Iowa grands' 0.2–2 ¢, each repeating to about 1 ¢ or better across captures; synthetic notes on the law come back within 0.01 ¢ through the same chain. Partials of different keys at the same frequency share their offsets, the signature of bridge and soundboard coupling (Gough 1981, *Acustica* 49); the rest may be the winding. On piano #2 the string lands a median 1–3 ¢ off target at partials 4–8, and which partial is loudest decides how far. Candidate: references from the capture's measured partials, f₁*·f_n/f₁ of its own fit, which zero the fitted series whatever partial is shown. No peer-reviewed study tests this on a strobe, so first a lab replay of where each capture's string lands under each rule, and a retune test: one string captured at two tunings about 10 ¢ apart, its measured ratios compared.
- **What engine (d) balances, and how smoothly** — `Investigating`. With B from the fitted curve, engine (d) still takes two things from the captures: which intervals exist (both coincident partials heard) and how much each counts (Giordano's sensitivity, the two partials' measured loudness times the roughness slope at their frequency, a published perceptual model unverified for this use). Its smoothness λ is picked by leave-one-row-out cross-validation, a tool for noisy data. The fitted curve's targets carry no noise, so the score is nearly flat, 6–18 % across λ = 10² to 10⁴ on the six complete profiles (balanced; 6–217 % pure twelfths), and the pick moves a step whenever the set of intervals changes, which moves A0 by up to 1.4 ¢ on a complete profile and up to 17 ¢ on a partial one. Beyond the smoothing the curve needs, λ only pulls the bass toward engine (a): on piano #2, A0 sits at −17 ¢ with just enough smoothing, −20 ¢ at the cross-validated pick, and −50 ¢ under engine (a). With no smoothing, today's inputs kink the curve, up to 8 ¢ of second difference in piano #2's bass. With stated weights and every interval, the kinks shrink to about 1 ¢, but the balance then honours coincidences of partials the top notes do not produce and puts C8 18 ¢ higher, so it needs a rule for which coincidences count in each register. Such a rule would also make treble captures optional: today the treble's rows come only from captures, and on piano #2 leaving out every capture from A4 up moves the default curve's top octave by up to 2.6 ¢ (it eases back to engine (a)), while which stored capture each key reads moves it by at most 0.15 ¢ (SD over 24 draws). Open together: where the weights come from (a stated table, or fitted to well-tuned pianos), which coincidences count, and what, if anything, is smoothed (the progression of interval widths, as tuners listen for, rather than a pull toward engine (a)). The first step is descriptive: how the recordings Rigaud assumed well tuned (RWC #2 and #3, the Iowa grand) were tuned, then the engines against them. The engines' own disagreement is larger: on piano #2 the shipped app puts A0 at −50.4 ¢ under (a), −19.6 ¢ under (d) balanced and −14.1 ¢ under pure twelfths, and which curve to offer still waits on an aurally tuned instrument (*When an in-tune instrument is captured*).
  → [report 0008](reports/0008-giordano-layer-fidelity-derived-weights.md), [report 0024](reports/0024-curve-fitted-b-default.md)
  → [report 0008](reports/0008-giordano-layer-fidelity-derived-weights.md)
- **Does a bass model with more freedom than two asymptotes describe every piano?** — `Investigating`. Rigaud's bass asymptote fits the bass keys of the upright, the Disklavier and the RWC Yamaha and Steinway within 0.3 in ln B, but misses the RWC Bösendorfer's, whose bass is nearly flat, by up to 0.45, about a factor of 1.5 either way. A break at the wound-string boundary, a third parameter or a smoothed per-key residual may describe it; it matters for the tuning curve, where cents count, more than for discovery, where the fit was non-inferior.
  → [report 0021](reports/0021-curve-seeded-templates.md)
- **`instrument_b_fit` is not identifiable from keys off the bass bridge** — resolved as one fit for both: the curve engines take the templates' pair, `curves::bass_bridge_fit` (points at twice the treble term or more, the slope held under twelve keys of them), and `instrument_b_fit` is gone. A profile measured only above the bass now leaves the curve's bass on the default pair; fitted on every measured key, such profiles had put A0 at 0.27× to 160× the default.
  → [report 0021](reports/0021-curve-seeded-templates.md), [report 0022](reports/0022-curve-bass-bridge-fit.md)
- **The bass-bridge fit takes each reading at face value** — `Planned`, after the model-B landing and the MAT bounds change. Two hard rules choose its points and its slope: a key counts at twice the treble term or more, and the slope is fitted once the points span twelve keys, lowest to highest. A reading that is wrong, or high because the piano's treble is stiffer than Rigaud's universal treble, passes the first and, far above the bass, switches on the second: on a profile with two bass captures, piano #2's E6 misread (2.3× the treble term, 9 partials) moves the curve by up to 2.2 ¢. Keys from A4 up are kept out meanwhile; C3–G♯4 stays open, with no such reading among its 1,058 across the nine capture sets. The replacement: an estimate under Rigaud's model with each key weighted by its bass share of B (near 1 in the low bass, under 4 % at A4), a heavy-tailed noise model from the partial-count law, and a prior on the bass pair from the reference pianos in place of the span rule; judged by thinning complete profiles and injecting the misreads seen, with the criteria fixed in advance. The A4 cut then goes.
  → [report 0022](reports/0022-curve-bass-bridge-fit.md), [report 0024](reports/0024-curve-fitted-b-default.md)
- **What replaces an excluded measurement** — moot for the app since report 0024: the default curve takes every key's B from the fitted curve, so the blend's negative-stretch exclusion changes no target, and the red ✗ reads the physical range instead. The exclusion still runs inside the blend that engines (b) and (c) read; its unexamined faults matter only if the blend returns: it falls back to the model rather than to the key's earlier trusted capture, and a session started mid-compass can exclude a correct key whose octave partner still carries the prior.
  → [report 0024](reports/0024-curve-fitted-b-default.md)
- **MAT's pair bounds have no derivation** — `Deferred`. `B_MIN` (−1e-3) and `B_MAX` (0.05) are ours (the paper has no B bounds) and only drop mis-numbered pairs before the median; neither bounds the string's B. Both do work: without the floor readings across the compass fall to 0, and without the ceiling treble readings drift away from their repeats. Neither value is derived. Replacements: a floor from each pair's own frequency precision (how far frequency error can push a correctly numbered pair below zero), and a ceiling per key from the string formula with bounding inputs (Conklin 1996, Part III, Eq. A18).
- **Remove MAT's confidence score** — `Planned`, with the MAT bounds change (decided 2026-11-08). `MatEstimate::confidence` is ours, not the paper's: pairwise self-consistency, scaled by how many pairs backed the median. It is not accuracy (a coherent wrong series, such as an octave mis-seed giving 4×B, scores high), nothing in the app reads it, and as a bad-capture detector it was measured and rejected (*The bad-capture check is one-sided detector*, under Known limitations). What still uses it: the Worker writes it into every capture's `analysis.json` as `b_confidence`, and `cargo lab mat validate`, `mat regen` and `mat recovery` print it. Removing it changes the dump format and those modes' output; keeping it costs a field whose docs must keep saying what it is not.
  → [report 0006](reports/0006-discovery-refinement-validation.md) (item 4)
- **MAT serial-vs-simultaneous on an in-tune instrument** — `Gated on` an in-tune instrument (see *When an in-tune instrument is captured*). Confirming the serial order generalizes retires the simultaneous fallback and re-enables the paper's tighter §2.4 peak-detection band. The deletion itself waits for the `(f₀, B)` seam, which injects `MatOrder` as a parameter.
- **MAT $f_0$ vs the tracked $f_0$** — `Deferred` (by design, recorded). The Worker reports the Goertzel-tracked $f_0$ as `measured_f0`; MAT's jointly refined $f_0$ is diagnostic only. Final $B$ accuracy awaits an in-tune instrument.
- **Soundboard fingerprint** — `Deferred`, not yet investigated. A soundboard, room or undamped-string resonance sits at the same frequency whatever key is struck, so a per-instrument spectral fingerprint could mask it during MAT's partial association. Report 0013 §5 leaves two candidates: 170.7 Hz on piano #1 and 145.6 Hz on piano #2, each seen through several partials of many keys and above its null. A third reaches MAT directly: on piano #2 a line at 53.8–54.0 Hz is G1's listed partial 1 in all nine captures (the reading puts the fundamental at 48.8 Hz), and it recurs in other keys' captures far more often than in control windows. MAT's median absorbs it (B moves under 1 %).
  → [report 0013](reports/0013-bass-extra-lines-attribution.md)
- **A physical piano simulation for testing MAT** — `Deferred`, unscheduled. Synthetic test notes today are sums of partials on the single-B law, while real bass partials sit off it (*The strobe's bass targets assume one B per string*) and MAT's failures come from lines the strings alone do not make. A simulation in Python, in four stages: the stiff string and hammer (exact B, a weak fundamental); the bridge with a modal soundboard (shifted low partials, soundboard lines); unison strings on that bridge; the nonlinear string (phantom partials), with the rotating bridge of Castera et al. 2023 (INRIA RR-9530) so they come out at realistic strength. Output: the pressure at one flat-response microphone through per-mode radiation responses (the Rayleigh integral), the air's back-reaction folded into the modal damping. Inputs: Chabassier & Duruflé's Steinway D set (INRIA RT-0425), the upright soundboard of Ege, Boutillon & Rébillat (JSV 332, 2013), and piano #2's captures with its measured speaking lengths (f₀ and B per key fix each wound string's tension and stiffness; an outer diameter says nothing). Numerics: energy-based schemes, and for the nonlinear string the quadratized schemes of Castera & Chabassier 2023 (INRIA RR-9516) with its convergence checks. First, a survey of the coupling and dissipation literature, which Bank & Chabassier 2019 call unsettled.

## Known limitations

These are measured and understood, not defects awaiting a fix.

- **Noisy environments.** Sympathetic noise is filtered by a −30 dB relative amplitude mask; below ~30 dB SNR, noise bleeds past it and can destabilize detection.
- **DC blocker corner sits at 35 Hz, above A0.** Measured and deliberately kept: raising it is actively harmful, and a steeper filter changes MAT's $B$ by a median of 0.00 %. Reopens only if a consumer starts using the bottom octave's fundamental.
  → [report 0019](reports/retiring/0019-dc-blocker-corner.md)
- **Stereo DC blocker (latent).** Unreachable through `open_input_stream`, which accepts mono `f32` configs only; live only for `AudioSource::External`.
- **The bad-capture check is one-sided.** The red ✗ marks a capture whose B is above the physical range of piano strings (report 0024). A B too low is not flagged, since in the treble that is missing information a recapture cannot mend, and nothing compares a capture with its neighbours. Two other candidates were measured and rejected: MAT's `b_confidence` (self-consistency, not accuracy — report 0006 item 4) and a σ_lnB repeat-disagreement threshold (fires on 29 of 88 well-behaved keys, and is weakly correlated with how much the disagreement moves the target). Anything subtler is caught by eye, through the measurement inspector.
- **Coarse readout caveats.** Bounded and measured — the search loss correction, the register where the gate degenerates to a ratio test, and the motion tail.
  → [report 0011](reports/0011-coarse-spectral-readout.md)
- **Unison assist is resolution-bound, and says so.** A split resolves only once it clears `2/T`; at or below the limit the *reported* separation collapses onto the limit rather than the truth, and it is unreliable either way out to ≈1.6 ×. The panel therefore always states what the current record is worth. 1.5 s of observation cannot reach the endgame (a set unison is well under 1 ¢), which is a property of observation time and not of the estimator.
  → [report 0012](reports/0012-unison-line-estimator.md) §4
- **The stated resolution is geometric, and over-promises for a quiet second string.** Measured: two strings within 6 dB resolve at the limit itself, but one 12 dB or more down needs ≈1.6 × it. The panel does not qualify the number, because the condition is unobservable precisely when it bites — the corner is the one where the second line is *not* found, so there is no second amplitude to condition on. Closing it is an estimator change (single-kernel fit, asymmetric-shoulder residual), not a display one.
  → [report 0012](reports/0012-unison-line-estimator.md) §4

## No ETA

- **CPAL/ALSA shutdown workaround, unverified against current CPAL.** The GUI sets `exit_on_close_request: false` and `HostHandle::stop()` joins the analysis thread before the stream drops. Both were the fix for a shutdown segfault on Linux/ALSA, and there has been none since. Whether current CPAL still needs them is untested, and testing it means deliberately reintroducing a crash on exit — so it stays as is until something gives a reason to look.
  → `tuner-gui/src/app.rs`, `tuner-core/src/audio.rs` (`HostHandle::stop`)
- **Analysis-thread start-up delay, untested.** `audio::spawn_analysis_thread` sleeps 100 ms before its first read, commented as letting the GUI initialize, and has since the first commit. `tuner-core` cannot see a GUI, so either something at start-up still depends on the delay or it does nothing. Remove it and watch the first frames after launch and a capture armed straight away.
- **Treble capture-window placement.** Treble upper partials run 20–45 dB stronger before the Golden Window opens than inside it, so up there the gatekeeper may be selecting the least informative segment of the note. Offline test against the full-event dumps; a negative result closes it.
  → [report 0009](reports/0009-repeat-capture-noise-decomposition.md) analysis 7
- **Curve comparison metrics.** The offline `cargo lab curve compare` harness already computes beat-rate smoothness, leave-keys-out prediction error, Giordano cross-scoring and curvature. Surfacing them in-GUI is held because the comparison semantics are themselves a research question — there is no ground-truth-free "best" curve.
- **A threshold for curve differences that matter.** Nothing in the project says how large a difference between two tuning curves a listener can hear, or a tuner would bother to correct. So every comparison of curves reports cents without saying whether they matter, and a rule that compares curves has no margin to stand on. A psychoacoustic threshold, from the literature or a listening test, would anchor every such margin.
- **Register-aware gatekeeper gate.** The A/B in audit 05 found the paper's sparsity core wins bass/mid while ours wins treble decisively. A register-aware gate is a candidate upgrade. The A/B has run on one instrument; running it on piano #2 (`cargo lab gatekeeper sparsity diagnostics_piano2`) is what the gate waits on. Tonality discrimination does not depend on tuning, so this needs no in-tune instrument.
  → [audit 05](reports/retiring/faithfulness-audit-05-metrics.md)
- **Interval-beat strobe.** Build-if-requested, not planned: intervals are correct by construction once every note sits on the curve.
